Vocal range.

Reading the ranges

Ranges are sounding pitch, written in scientific pitch notation (middle C is C4). Sheet music writes male vocal parts an octave higher than they sound, so Musicnotes lists Iris as D4–A5 while this list says D3–A4.

Most ranges come from Singing Carrots' crowd-sourced data, so treat them as estimates and check important songs with a pitch monitor.

Bar key: the light band is your full reach, the darker band is your working range, and the hatched part is your upper extension. The outlined line is the song's full range in the original key. In the key shown, the thin tinted bar is the full range and the thick bar is the tessitura: the middle 80% of singing time, where the song actually sits.

Tessitura comes from the melody track of a MIDI file, weighting each note by how long it's held. It's only used when the file's melody matched the song's known range. Songs without one use the middle of their range instead, and the best key centres that note in your sweet spot. Measured only hides them.

Best key mostly looks at the tessitura: it moves the song so the bulk of the singing sits between two semitones above your working bottom and two below your comfortable top. The lowest and highest notes must stay between your floor and ceiling, and brief peaks above your comfortable top count against a key only a little. It prefers the smallest change of key, and singing an octave down counts as the original key.

Vocal health

If one song leaves you very tired, you're forcing the extreme notes. A song you can only finish once isn't really in your range.

Stop for the day if you notice: hoarseness, a voice still tired the next morning, pain, high notes dropping out, constant throat-clearing, or creeping breathiness. See an ENT if hoarseness lasts more than two weeks.

  • Warm up with straw phonation, lip trills, or humming on "ng" for 5–10 minutes.
  • Use less air pressure on high notes.
  • Narrow vowels as you go up ("ah" toward "uh", "ee" toward "ih").
  • Before starting a song, check the first note on a tuner that shows octave numbers.
